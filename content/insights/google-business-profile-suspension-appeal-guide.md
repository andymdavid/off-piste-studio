## Start with the confirmed restriction

Before sending evidence, keep the suspension notice, affected profile name and Google account together in a private incident record. If your business simply isn't appearing and you haven't confirmed a restriction, start by [diagnosing why your profile isn't showing](/insights/google-business-profile-not-showing-diagnostic-guide).

[Google explains the effects of profile and account removal](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145). A removed profile is inaccessible to the public and its managers. An owner's account removal affects owned locations, while a manager's account removal affects that manager's access without affecting the locations. Check which decision applies before deciding how to appeal.

If the account itself is restricted, Google's guidance directs you to appeal the account through your Google Account first. Once that restriction is lifted, follow the available profile appeal route. Don't create a replacement profile for the same business.

## Check the decision and business facts

Sign in with the account associated with the profile and review the decision in the [Google Business Profile appeals tool](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551). Google says the tool can show the decision date, reason, policy link, appeal eligibility and status. Record what the tool says. A general policy label doesn't tell you which undisclosed fact caused the restriction.

Read the linked policy alongside [Google's guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177). Check that the business is eligible and that its real-world name, category, address and service area are accurate. Correct demonstrable inaccuracies where the account allows it, and record what changed and why. For a business that visits customers, check the [service-area and address settings](/insights/google-business-profile-service-area-business-guide) against how it actually operates. The broader [profile optimisation guide](/insights/google-business-profile-guide) covers the surrounding setup.

## Prepare evidence that answers the policy issue

Build a small, relevant evidence pack before opening the submission flow. Google's [suspension guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145) gives registration, business licences, tax certificates and business utility bills as examples, and asks that the business name and address on documents match the profile. Choose evidence that addresses the stated issue. Explain legitimate differences, such as a trading name or recent move, and use supporting records where available.

Keep documents private and share them only through Google's indicated channels. Check each file for customer information and other sensitive details that aren't needed for the review. Keep an internal record of the documents supplied and who can access them.

For example, a fictional mobile locksmith operates from a private base and visits customers. Its evidence pack might include its business registration, a relevant licence and a business utility bill connecting the operation to its base, with a short explanation of the trading name and service-area model. The owner would check that these facts agree with the profile and the policy notice. This is an example of preparation, not a prediction of Google's decision.

## Submit the available appeal

For businesses outside the UK and EEA, including Australia, [Google describes a profile suspension appeal](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551) through its appeals tool. Follow the sequence in the guidance.

1. Sign in to the Google account associated with the profile and select the affected profile.
2. Select the decision and check its policy, status and eligibility for appeal.
3. If offered, add relevant evidence and a concise explanation connecting the documents to the decision.
4. Review the details and attachments, confirm you're ready, and submit. Save the submission date and any case reference.

Google says an appeal can only be submitted once. Make the initial submission as complete and accurate as you can, and don't send multiple appeals for the same issue while awaiting a decision. Google's published instructions currently describe attaching optional files and context in the tool. Your account's screen and evidence instructions may differ, so follow the prompts you see. We haven't inspected an affected Australian account and can't confirm its particular interface or deadline.

## Track the decision and choose the next step

Sign in to the appeals tool with the associated account to check the status, and watch for Google's decision email. Record the outcome and any instructions. If the tool offers no appeal and you believe there's an eligible restriction, use the contact option in [Google's appeal guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551) to ask support about eligibility.

If reinstatement is denied, [Google says an additional review may be available](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145) and can include evidence not provided with the original appeal. Check the current instructions and make any new evidence relevant to the decision. An additional review and reinstatement aren't guaranteed.

If Google asks you to verify the business, treat that as a separate task. [Available verification methods depend on the business and other factors](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242). If video is offered, use the [verification video preparation guide](/insights/google-business-profile-video-verification-guide) to plan the recording. Once the profile is active and normal activity resumes, [measure qualified leads from the profile](/insights/measure-google-business-profile-performance-qualified-leads) over time rather than assuming visibility has recovered immediately.

## Keep business information consistent

Use the incident record to check the same real-world facts on your website and other maintained business listings. Assign someone to update those details when the business moves, changes its trading name or changes how it serves customers. For complex inconsistencies across locations and pages, [SEO support](/services/seo) can help coordinate the factual cleanup. Google decides eligibility and the outcome of any appeal.
