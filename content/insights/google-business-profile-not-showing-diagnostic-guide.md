## First identify the visibility symptom

"My Google Business Profile isn't showing" can describe several different problems. The profile might be absent from Search and Maps, visible to its owner but not the public, marked as a duplicate, restricted, still processing, or easy to find by business name but absent for service searches.

Each symptom needs a different remedy. An ownership conflict needs an access request, while another profile can make a duplicate problem worse. A wider service area still leaves the searcher's distance unchanged.

Start by writing down what you expected to see and what you actually saw. Include the exact business name or search query, the location of the person searching, whether they used Search or Maps, and whether they were signed in. Google's guidance on [finding your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en) also distinguishes the profile an owner can manage from the information customers can see.

If the profile exists and needs routine work on categories, service areas, reviews, posts, or completeness, use our [Google Business Profile optimisation guide](/insights/google-business-profile-guide). Stay with this diagnostic when visibility itself is the unresolved question.

## Check whether the business is eligible and verified

Eligibility is the first hard gate. Under Google's current [Business Profile eligibility guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en), a business generally needs to make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours. Google defines limited exceptions. It lists online-only businesses and lead-generation companies among the businesses that aren't eligible.

An ineligible operation can't solve the issue by adding more information or choosing a different category. It needs another search strategy built around an eligible website and other appropriate channels.

For an eligible business, check verification next. Google says [only verified businesses can show the business information they manage](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en) on Search and Maps. Being able to open an owner interface does not prove that the public profile is verified and visible.

The information must also represent the real operation. Google's [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) require an accurate real-world name, address or service area, core categories, and one profile per business except where specific rules permit another. Use the verification route Google offers for the profile and make sure the business owner retains appropriate ownership.

## Check the profile state before changing anything

Once eligibility and verification are clear, inspect the current state and recent history. Record any change to the name, ownership, address, service area, category, website, or verification. Save the relevant notice or email without placing private evidence in a public forum.

A new profile or recent edit may still be processing. Google's [profile visibility guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en) says rankings for a new business can take up to a month to appear and edits can take up to three days to affect search results. These are processing estimates rather than visibility guarantees. Repeated speculative edits during that period can remove the clean baseline you need for diagnosis.

If the profile is suspended or disabled, Google says it won't show to other users until the issue is resolved. Review the policy notice, correct any substantiated issue, gather the requested evidence, and use Google's [Business Profile appeals process](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551?hl=en-GB). Don't guess at an enforcement reason that Google hasn't stated and don't create a replacement profile to evade the restriction.

A duplicate needs its own route. Google says a profile it considers a duplicate [won't appear on Search or Maps](https://support.google.com/business/answer/12756178?hl=en). An existing profile owned by somebody else calls for an ownership request. Two profiles for the same business may call for removal or a merge request. Two distinct and eligible businesses that were incorrectly merged may need an appeal with evidence that they are genuinely separate.

## Test Search and Maps with controlled conditions

One manual search is useful evidence, but it can't establish visibility across a market. Local results change with the query and the searcher's location. Signed-in state, device, and the Search or Maps surface can also change what you observe.

Use a repeatable sequence.

1. Search the exact business name and city in Google Search, then repeat it in Google Maps.
2. Record the date, device, signed-in state, and physical or simulated location used for the check.
3. Repeat with one precise service query a genuine customer would use. Keep the location and other conditions unchanged.
4. Note whether the profile appears, where it appears, and whether another listing or duplicate appears instead.
5. Compare the observation with Business Profile performance, Search Console, website analytics, and qualified enquiry records over the same period.

Use several dated observations before calling a movement a loss. A single rank-grid point has the same limitation. It samples one query from one coordinate at one moment.

This is Off Piste's diagnostic interpretation of Google's documented model. Google says local results are mainly based on [relevance, distance, and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en), which means a search result observed from one place cannot stand in for every customer location.

## Separate branded visibility from discovery visibility

If the profile appears for the exact business name, you have evidence that it exists publicly. If it remains absent for relevant service searches, the question has moved from profile existence to local discovery.

Relevance describes how well the profile matches the search. Accurate categories and detailed business information can improve Google's understanding. Distance describes how far the business is from the searcher. A wider service area tells customers where the business works, but it does not move the business or override distance. Prominence reflects how well known the business is, with Google citing signals such as links and reviews.

Google also states that there is [no way to request or pay for a better organic local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). No category, review target, service-area setting, profile edit, or SEO engagement can guarantee a position.

Check that the profile accurately describes the operation, then use the [profile optimisation guide](/insights/google-business-profile-guide) for the field-level work. If the profile is already complete and accurate, investigate the website, public business evidence, competition, and measurement instead of repeatedly rewriting the same listing.

## Compare visibility with business outcomes

The commercial question is larger than whether one person saw a map result. You need to know whether relevant exposure changed and whether that change affected enquiries.

Google's [Business Profile performance definitions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en) cover applicable measures such as search terms, views on Search and Maps, calls, website clicks, directions, messages, and bookings. Metric availability varies by profile and feature. A view is exposure. A call or click is an interaction. Neither proves that a qualified lead or sale occurred.

Compare several periods and keep seasonality, campaigns, closures, and major profile or website changes in view. Search Console can show whether local landing pages lost search exposure. Analytics can show what profile visitors did after reaching the website. Google also documents a [Business Profile connection to Google Analytics](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/16930347), subject to the current account and property setup. Your CRM or enquiry log provides the final check on lead quality.

The repair should follow the failure the evidence supports. The loudest metric in one interface may point somewhere else.

## Repair the system the evidence points to

An ownership, verification, duplicate, restriction, or pending-edit state has a profile-level remedy. Follow the official route for that state and preserve a dated record of submissions and responses. Extra optimisation work should wait until the profile is eligible, verified, unrestricted, and publicly visible.

When names, locations, ownership, phone numbers, or other public facts conflict across platforms, [audit the conflicting third-party business signals](/insights/third-party-brand-signals-ai-search-audit). Contradictory information can make the business harder for customers and search systems to identify consistently.

If the website presents unclear or conflicting organisation facts, [govern those facts and their structured representation](/insights/organization-schema-service-business-guide). Schema can support machine-readable website information. Google does not document it as a direct Business Profile ranking lever.

If the profile is healthy and the evidence shows weak landing pages, unclear service relevance, limited authority, or poor conversion tracking, the issue belongs to [the wider local search system](/insights/how-to-get-on-first-page-of-google). That investigation should connect profile exposure with the website and qualified outcomes.

## Decide when the problem needs strategic SEO work

Many incidents can remain self-serve. An eligible business can complete verification. An owner can request access. A duplicate or restriction can follow Google's official resolution route. A recent edit can be monitored against the documented processing window.

The threshold changes when an eligible, verified, unrestricted profile is live, yet visibility remains weak across valuable service searches and the evidence crosses query relevance, geography, website quality, authority, competition, and lead attribution. At that point, the diagnosis has expanded into a search-system problem that extends beyond a single profile state.

When the problem reaches that threshold, [strategic SEO support](/services/seo) should start with the evidence record, identify what limits qualified discovery, and set measures tied to enquiries and revenue. No engagement can promise a local ranking position.
