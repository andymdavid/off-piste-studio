## Start with the buyer question behind the keyword

You want to plan content around a valuable search, but the keyword doesn't tell you everything the buyer needs to know.

In this illustrative journey, imagine someone asks Google AI Mode, "How do I choose an SEO partner for a growing service business in Perth if I need leads, reporting and help fixing the website?"

They're testing service fit, location, process, proof, measurement, website capability, risk and the next step. One oversized article would struggle to answer all of those jobs well. Eight thin articles would create a different problem.

Map the questions behind the buyer's decision, identify the best page and evidence for each one, then decide what needs to change.

Our guide to [how Google AI Mode affects search visibility](/insights/google-sge-and-seo) explains the wider platform.

## What query fan-out changes in content planning

Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use [query fan-out across related subtopics and data sources](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). Its [generative AI optimisation guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) describes concurrent related queries that retrieve more information for the original request.

A single complex search can create several opportunities for Google to retrieve supporting information. A relevant service page might answer the service-fit question, while a pricing guide could explain costs. A case study or independent review might provide proof.

Query families inferred from tools, search results or workshops are planning hypotheses. Google's exact fan-out queries remain hidden, and mapped coverage can't guarantee page selection.

Normal Search foundations still apply. Google's [AI feature eligibility requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) say a supporting page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet. The same requirements apply to AI supporting links.

## Choose one commercially important research journey

Start with a question tied to a real buyer decision. Sales calls, enquiry forms, Search Console queries and customer interviews are better raw material than a brainstorm built only from keyword modifiers.

For the illustrative SEO partner question, the buyer has a credible service need and a clear commercial next step. Off Piste can also support the answer with service details, an approach to reporting, website capability and relevant proof. That makes the journey worth mapping.

A broad question such as "What is SEO?" would be a weaker starting point. It tells you little about the buyer’s decision or the evidence they need.

Test the question against four conditions. It should reflect a real customer concern, connect to a service you can deliver, require evidence you can defend and lead naturally to a useful next action. Expand it into a content programme only when it meets those conditions.

The [decision-stage content guide](/insights/search-intent-decision-stage-content-guide) helps you judge buyer value and choose the right page type. Use that process before treating a related search as a publishing opportunity.

## Map the likely retrieval jobs

Break the decision into research jobs you can map to pages. For the SEO partner example, useful families might include service fit, cost, comparison, process, proof, risk, location and next step.

Include a location branch when local knowledge or service availability changes the decision. Include cost when scope and budget shape the choice. If pricing requires discovery, explain what determines the scope.

Build your hypotheses from several kinds of evidence:

- questions prospects ask in sales calls, emails and forms
- the pages and result formats appearing for the main question
- People Also Ask and related searches
- language used in reviews, forums and customer interviews
- Search Console queries that already expose adjacent needs
- SEO or AI visibility tools, when their outputs are treated as prompts for investigation

Record the source and confidence beside each family. You can give more weight to a repeated sales objection backed by a stable results pattern than to one suggestion from a tool.

Google's optimisation guidance recommends [valuable, non-commodity content instead of separate pages for every possible fan-out variation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). More URLs can spread authority, proof and maintenance effort across weaker pages.

## Match each job to a page and evidence role

For each useful family, record the best current page, the buyer's intent, the page's role, the evidence it needs, the links around it and its conversion destination.

The SEO service page can own fit, scope and the primary enquiry path. A decision guide can explain how to compare providers and what changes cost. A case study can carry first-hand proof. A Perth page can explain genuine local relevance. Descriptive internal links connect these roles.

Assign one primary URL to each material job. If a service page and article compete for ownership, use our guide to [choosing the right page to target a keyword](/insights/service-page-vs-blog-post-search-intent-guide) to resolve the format and intent before adding another URL.

Match the evidence to the claim. If you say a process improved qualified enquiries, you need a documented case with an attributable outcome. A statement about Google's platform needs a current primary source. A claim about reputation may need independent reviews or coverage rather than more first-party copy.

Our guide to [making content worth citing in AI search](/insights/ai-search-citation-worthy-content) explains how to match evidence to a claim. If you don't know which important claims are supported, start with a [website evidence audit](/insights/website-content-evidence-audit).

## Decide whether to keep, refresh, consolidate, connect or create

Our keep, refresh, consolidate, connect and create method turns the map into editorial decisions about existing pages and the few assets worth creating. These are Off Piste’s planning decisions. Google doesn't prescribe this workflow.

| Current state | Finding | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong page | It answers the job with current evidence | Keep and monitor |
| Weak page | Its role is right but the answer or proof is thin | Refresh the page |
| Duplicate pages | Several URLs compete for the same job | Consolidate them |
| Complementary pages | The answers exist but the journey is broken | Connect them with useful links |
| Missing page | No page owns a material buyer decision | Create one focused asset |
| External proof gap | The claim needs independent support | Seek credible corroboration |

Keep a page when it already answers the buyer’s question. Refresh it when it has the right role but needs better scope, clarity or evidence. Consolidate when several pages repeat the same answer without distinct roles. Connect when useful pages exist but readers have no obvious route between them.

Create a page only when the missing job is material and deserves a durable destination. A substantial provider-comparison guide might earn its own URL. A narrow question about monthly reporting may fit better as a section on the service page.

Seek third-party corroboration when independent evidence carries the claim. Reviews, professional credentials, partner listings and credible coverage can support reputation or identity claims more convincingly than first-party copy alone.

A stronger connection between pages or better evidence can resolve a coverage gap within the existing site. Compare the mapped query families with the existing site through the [decision-stage content gap analysis](/insights/decision-stage-content-gap-analysis) before turning them into a publishing plan.

## Strengthen structure and evidence where the role is right

A correctly assigned page can still be hard to use. The heading structure may hide the main answer. Important service facts may sit inside images. Proof may be separated from the claim it supports. The next action may be unclear.

Google recommends useful visible text, crawlability, a good page experience and structured data that matches the page. Its [generative AI optimisation guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) also rejects AI-only tricks such as special markup or forced content chunking.

Use the [structured content guide](/insights/structured-content-ai-search-guide) when a page has the right role but readers struggle to find its answers. Improve headings, visible answers, descriptive links and the placement of proof for people first.

Organisation schema can clarify consistent identity facts when it accurately matches the visible page. If identity is one of the mapped jobs, our [organisation schema guide](/insights/organization-schema-service-business-guide) explains the narrower implementation role.

Sometimes the constraint is bigger than copy. Navigation, templates and proof components can prevent several pages from working together. That belongs in the [website design](/services/website-design) plan because the system needs to change alongside the content.

## Build internal links around the buyer's next question

Internal links should follow the research journey. Place each link where the reader naturally needs the next answer, and describe what they'll find.

An SEO service page might link to a provider-comparison guide where a buyer is evaluating fit. That guide might link to a case study beside an outcome claim and return to the service page when the reader is ready to discuss scope. A local page might link to the same service without duplicating the entire offer.

If your map reveals overlapping pages, broken journeys or unclear ownership, [SEO strategy and content architecture](/services/seo) can help decide what to consolidate and how to connect the remaining pages.

## Validate the map after implementation

Validation starts with what you can observe. Test whether the pages are eligible, whether your hypotheses still match the results and whether visibility and buyer behaviour change. Google's exact hidden fan-out queries remain outside that evidence.

Start with crawlability, indexing and snippet eligibility. Review the relevant AI Mode and AI Overview answers manually, while allowing for location, history and result variation. Check whether the answer is accurate, whether your pages appear as supporting sources and whether competitors expose a research job your map missed.

Google’s [Generative AI performance report Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) records worldwide rollout from 31 August 2026. The same date appears in the update to Google’s [website-owner announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-controls-website-owners/). Worldwide availability doesn't ensure that your property will show a report or contain useful data. Google says insufficient generative impressions can explain a missing report, and exclusion from generative AI features can affect eligibility. Use our guide to [the Search Console generative AI performance report](/insights/google-search-console-generative-ai-report-guide) for setup, dimensions and availability troubleshooting.

Each evidence source answers a different question. Indexing and snippet checks establish eligibility. Manual answer reviews record which sources appeared in that particular check. Generative impressions show recorded exposure for your pages in Google’s AI features. None provides a trace of the hidden fan-out query set or establishes why a page was selected. Keep your inferred query families labelled as hypotheses when comparing them with these observations.

Compare generative impressions with conventional Search queries and landing pages, referral signals, engagement, enquiries and lead quality. Our full guide to [measuring AI search visibility](/insights/how-to-measure-ai-search-visibility) shows how to keep exposure separate from commercial outcomes. An increase in impressions alone doesn't tell you whether the mapped pages helped a suitable buyer enquire.

Independent research gives another reason to check supporting sources alongside conventional rankings. Xu, Iqbal and Montgomery’s [Measuring Google AI Overviews, version two](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14021v2), revised on 1 October 2026 and accepted to ACM IMC 2026, studied 55,393 trending queries across 19 categories from 13 March to 21 April 2026, a 40-day window. Nearly 30 per cent of AI Overview-cited domains were absent from the co-displayed first-page results in that sample. The trending-query sample and observation window limit what you can infer about Australian service-business searches or AI Mode. The finding justifies checking actual supporting sources alongside rankings. It doesn't establish citation factors or prove that a page change caused a citation.

In an illustrative review on 13 November 2026, a marketing owner records that the provider-comparison guide is indexed, while a manual AI Mode check raises a reporting question that the guide leaves unanswered. The owner saves the indexing check and a dated record of the answer, including its location and wording. The hypothesis is that the existing guide needs a clearer explanation of reporting scope and a link to the service page. The omission's effect on source selection remains unknown. The marketing owner assigns the guide’s editor to make that focused repair and sets 11 December 2026 as the next review date, subject to enough reporting data becoming available. That review will compare the revised answer, repeated manual observations and enquiry quality before deciding whether any additional page is justified.

Set your own review date after a stable measurement period. Keep mapped pages whose roles and evidence still match the buyer’s journey. Refresh the assigned page when its answer is incomplete, consolidate overlapping ownership and create a page only when the review identifies a material research job without a suitable destination. Record the chosen action and owner in the map so the next review can assess a specific change.
