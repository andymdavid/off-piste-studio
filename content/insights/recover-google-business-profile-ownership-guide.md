## Start with the existing profile

Your Google Business Profile is a public record customers use to find and contact you. If a former employee or agency controls it, your business may struggle to correct its hours, phone number or other details. Control of the profile helps you maintain an accurate [representation of your business across search and AI](/insights/how-ai-search-understands-your-business).

First, sign in to the Google accounts your business uses and look for the existing profile. If you can manage it, open **Business Profile settings**, then **People and access**, to see its users and pending invitations. An account with manager access can work on many profile details, but owners control user access. [Google's guide to owner and manager permissions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en) explains the available roles.

Note the profile URL, business name and the account you used. Ask your current team and any former agency whether they know the owner. Don't create another profile for the same business while trying to recover this one.

A profile that isn't appearing may need a [visibility diagnosis](/insights/google-business-profile-not-showing-diagnostic-guide). A confirmed suspension has a separate [appeal process](/insights/google-business-profile-suspension-appeal-guide). If there's evidence of a copied identity or a misleading listing, use the [impersonation response guide](/insights/business-impersonation-search-maps-response-guide). An ownership dispute alone doesn't establish fraud. Google also provides a separate route to [report misleading business information](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/16109801).

## Arrange a cooperative handover

If the current owner is willing to help, ask them to invite a Google account the business controls as an owner. Each person should use their own account rather than sharing a login. The current owner can open **People and access**, select **Add**, enter the account email, choose **Owner** and send the invitation. Accept it and confirm that the account can access the correct profile. Google's [instructions for adding owners and managers](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en) include the current menu steps.

Next, arrange primary ownership for the business-controlled account. Google's [primary ownership transfer instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3415281?hl=en) direct the user to select the recipient in **People and access**, edit their access, choose **Primary owner** and save. Google says only the primary owner can transfer primary ownership, although the same page also contains a note about co-owners upgrading themselves. Follow the options available in the account and contact Google support if the role or transfer is unclear.

Allow for the waiting period: Google says a new owner or manager must wait seven days before using all features and may receive errors when removing users or changing primary ownership during that time. Confirm the business-controlled account has the intended access before removing a former agency or employee. The seven-day restriction is separate from the three-day response window for an ownership request.

A fictional café's former agency invites the café owner's business-controlled account. The owner accepts and checks the listing. After any applicable seven-day restriction, they complete and confirm the primary ownership change before removing the agency's access.

## Request ownership when access is unavailable

If you operate a storefront or hybrid business and can't arrange a handover, Google's [ownership request guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4566671?hl=en) sets out the following route for an existing verified profile.

1. Sign in with the Google account you want to use and go to [business.google.com/add](https://business.google.com/add).
2. Enter the business name and address, then choose the existing business from the list.
3. If Google says someone else may manage the profile, select **Request Access**.
4. Complete the form as an authorised representative and submit it. Keep the confirmation email and request details.

A service-area business that visits customers and has no physical shop or office follows a different route. Google says to [contact Business Profile support](https://support.google.com/business/gethelp), enter **Transfer ownership of listing** in the issue field and select that issue description when submitting the request. The interface and options may vary by account.

## Respond to Google's decision

Google says the current owner has **three days** to respond after an ownership request. Check the confirmation email or sign in to your Business Profile to see the request status. The next step depends on the response, as described in [Google's ownership guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4566671?hl=en).

- **Approved:** Google sends an approval email and you can manage the profile. Check your role and arrange primary ownership if needed.
- **Denied:** Google sends a rejection email. You can try to appeal the decision, or suggest an edit to the profile. Follow the options available to your account.
- **No response after three days:** Google may offer a claim option. Look for **View request** and **Verify** in the confirmation email, or a **Claim** or **Verify** button in the profile. Follow the prompts if offered. Google says the claim option isn't always available.

An ownership request doesn't guarantee a transfer or a particular verification method. If Google offers video verification, prepare using the [video verification guide](/insights/google-business-profile-video-verification-guide). If the route or result is unclear, use the support option in Google's guidance and keep a record of the request and replies.

## Keep control after access is restored

Give primary ownership to an account the business can manage over time. Keep a private register of the profile URL, account holders, roles, recovery contacts and the next review date. Review it when staff or suppliers change, and periodically after that. Give an agency only the access it needs and retain an owner account within the business.

[Google recommends limiting profile access and securing accounts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14509283). Use strong passwords and two-step verification, and remove access that’s no longer needed after confirming the remaining owners can manage the profile. Google says it won't ask for a one-time password or PIN and doesn't charge for profile verification or reinstatement. Be cautious of unsolicited offers that make those demands.

## Keep the wider business record accurate

Once access is secure, check the profile's public name, contact details, hours and website against your own records. Assign someone to investigate differences and make corrections. The [third-party business signals audit](/insights/third-party-brand-signals-ai-search-audit) can help you find other records that need attention, and the [Google Business Profile guide](/insights/google-business-profile-guide) covers routine improvements after recovery.

If several locations or supplier handovers make ownership difficult to track, [SEO support](/services/seo) can help organise an access and correction process around the people responsible for each profile.
