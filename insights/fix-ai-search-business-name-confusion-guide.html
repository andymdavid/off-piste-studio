Stop AI Search Confusing Your Business With Another Company

When another company’s services, reviews, people or reputation appear beside your business name, changing everything at once can make the problem harder to diagnose. Build a two-business evidence record, isolate the affected surfaces and correct the sources that own the errors.

September 27, 2026

Share

When another company appears inside your business story

A buyer searches your name and sees somebody else’s services. An AI answer attributes another company’s reviews to you. A founder, address or website from a namesake appears beside your brand. These aren’t minor presentation faults when they affect enquiries, trust or reputation.

This guide is for a specific problem. Two genuine organisations with identical or similar names are being merged or cross-attributed. If your business is simply described vaguely, omitted from answers or represented with old facts, begin with the broader guide to how AI search understands your business.

Namesake confusion can appear across several surfaces at once, or it can be confined to one label. Start by proving which facts are wrong, where they appear and which business each fact belongs to.

Prove the mix-up before changing anything

Create a small test set around the observed harm. Search the exact business name, then pair it with a location, core service and a disputed person or attribute. For generated answers, use prompts that ask directly about the fact being confused. Record the exact wording rather than paraphrasing it later.

For every test, note the platform, date, location and whether you were signed in. Save the returned claim, links or citations and a screenshot. Then write down the correct fact and the page or record that supports it. These details matter because generated answers and search results can vary between tests. One result is evidence of what appeared under those conditions, not proof of a system’s stable belief.

Keep the evidence capture compact enough to repeat. It will give you a fair comparison after each material correction.

In practice

Capture the confusion before you try to fix it

Keep the test conditions and the disputed facts together so later retests remain comparable.

  • Record the exact query or prompt
  • Note the platform, date, location and account state
  • Copy the wrong attribute and every cited source
  • Save a screenshot and the correct business fact
  • Repeat the same test after each material correction

Prioritise tests that expose commercial harm. A wrong service, phone number, location, review source or reputation claim deserves attention before a harmless logo variation. If a damaging statement appears, preserve the evidence before submitting feedback because the result may change independently.

Separate the two businesses on paper

Build one comparison that keeps the two organisations distinct. Use facts you can verify, not fields copied from the disputed result. This table becomes the reference for correcting profiles, briefing support teams and checking whether a cited page belongs to the right business.

Field Your business Other business
Official and trading names Current registered and public names Verified public names
Primary domain Canonical website URL Other organisation’s URL
Location Office, storefront or service area Its distinct location
Main services Current buyer-facing offer Its documented offer
People Founders, leaders and experts People attached to it
Identifiers ABN, ACN or other defensible IDs Its known identifiers
Official profiles Controlled social and platform URLs Profiles it appears to control
Reviews Platforms and profile URLs that belong to you Review sources that belong to it
Disputed sources Pages attaching its facts to you Pages attaching your facts to it

Unknown is a valid entry. Don’t fill a gap with an assumption about the other company. The comparison only needs enough evidence to show that the records describe two separate organisations and to identify which attributes have crossed over.

Find which surface is actually wrong

Start with the visible result type because similar-looking errors can have different owners.

An AI answer may assemble a response from several cited or retrieved pages. Capture the answer and inspect each available citation for the wrong attribute. Feedback can report the output, while the cited source may also need correction.

A Knowledge Panel is an entity summary, not the same product as a local Business Profile. Google’s verification guidance specifically recognises panels that are mixed with another entity of the same or a similar name. If the contamination is confined to that panel, use the focused Knowledge Panel correction route.

A Business Profile holds local operational details such as its category, hours, location and reviews. Check whether the wrong information is actually inside the profile or whether Google is showing a separate panel beside the result.

An organic site name is the label Google shows for a website in search results. Google says site names are generated automatically from homepage content and references on the web. It recommends a unique, consistent name and notes that the same site name generally won’t be used for two global sites. A wrong site label without mixed services, people or reviews belongs in the site-name repair guide.

Third-party directories and review platforms own their own records. A merged listing, wrong domain or misassigned review should be corrected at that source. Don’t assume changing your website will rewrite a record held elsewhere.

Trace the wrong attribute to its likely source

Take one contaminated fact at a time and search for the exact wording. Check your pages, structured data, Business Profile, official social accounts, major directories, review profiles and the pages cited in generated answers. A wrong fact may have begun in one record and then been repeated by others.

Create a source log with the wrong attribute, source URL, record owner, correction route and status. The third-party business signals audit provides a fuller inventory and prioritisation method once you know which attributes have crossed over.

Repeated agreement can make an error look well supported. Correct the source and its important copies, while treating any later change as an observed result rather than proof that a specific number of matching profiles caused it.

If the confusion centres on a founder, author or expert, separate the person from the organisation. Confirm their maintained profile, role, authored work and relevant external profiles. The expert profile and Person schema guide covers the implementation without turning a person into an alias for the business.

Strengthen the owned identity record

Your website should give a buyer one clear place to verify who the organisation is. A maintained organisation or about page can state the current name, primary domain, services, locations, key people, contact details and defensible identifiers in visible language. Link it naturally to relevant service, location and profile pages.

Google says Organization structured data can help it understand and disambiguate an organisation. Supported properties include name, alternateName, url, sameAs and identifiers. The markup should describe the visible, approved record. It can clarify your declaration. Corrections, rankings, citations and recommendations remain outside its control.

Use stable @id values and only connect profiles that genuinely represent the organisation. Don’t use sameAs for a directory category, an unrelated namesake or any page that doesn’t identify the same entity. Once the canonical facts are agreed, the Organization schema guide covers implementation and validation.

Correct the source that owns the error

Send each problem through the route for its surface. Submit an evidence-backed panel correction for a mixed Knowledge Panel. Correct an inaccurate third-party listing with its publisher. Repair homepage naming and WebSite signals for an isolated site-name problem. Report a generated answer through the platform’s available feedback route and include the evidence you preserved.

Keep Google Business Profile naming within policy. Google’s guidelines for representing a business require the name used consistently in the real world. Location, service area, category and service information belong in their designated fields. Don’t add keywords, locations or descriptors to the profile name solely to distinguish it in search.

Make one attributable set of corrections where practical, then record what changed. Changing the site, profiles, schema and business name together makes it difficult to know whether the source error was removed or merely hidden in one retest.

Know when the problem has moved beyond SEO

Australian business-name registration and search-platform identity resolution are different questions. ASIC can reject a proposed name it considers identical or nearly identical to an existing registered name. Registration still doesn’t establish how a search engine or AI system will separate two organisations online.

Questions about trademarks, passing off, disputed rights or consumer confusion need qualified legal advice. Preserve evidence of harm and avoid making claims about the other business’s intent. An SEO diagnosis can document the records and platform behaviour. Legal rights require legal advice.

A rename may be commercially sensible when confusion is persistent and costly. Treat it as an escalation choice. If the business chooses that path, use a governed rebrand and entity migration plan to maintain continuity across the domain, site, profiles, schema and external references.

Retest and record what changed

Repeat the original queries and prompts with the recorded location and account conditions. Track each surface independently. A Knowledge Panel may change while an AI answer still repeats an old citation, or a corrected directory may take time to appear elsewhere.

Record the result as changed, unchanged or mixed. Save new screenshots and citations. A clean result on one date is encouraging evidence, but it doesn’t prove a permanent fix. A change after several corrections also doesn’t establish which action caused it.

Continue monitoring when the wrong claim has disappeared across repeated checks and no high-risk source remains. Return to source correction when the same wrong attribute persists with a traceable owner. Escalate the diagnosis when several platforms conflict, the source can’t be isolated or the error is affecting leads and reputation.

When a joined-up diagnosis is worth it

Namesake confusion becomes expensive when teams chase every mention without a shared evidence record. A scoped SEO and AI visibility diagnosis can separate the two entities, map contaminated sources and sequence the surface-specific work around commercial risk.

Website work becomes relevant when templates, architecture or governance keep generating conflicting facts. In other cases, focus the repair on the source and surface identified by the evidence. The result is a defensible identity record, a correction trail and a repeatable test for returning confusion.
