## A shortened interview can lose the condition that matters

Imagine a service designer explaining when an enquiry form should be shorter. Their recommendation depends on whether the business actually uses the information it collects. A complex project may still need detailed questions before the team can assess fit.

An AI-generated post turns that into “Shorter forms always improve lead quality.” It removes the condition and adds a result the expert never claimed. A buyer could now expect an outcome that the interview doesn't support.

When you repurpose expert interviews with AI, keep every published derivative connected to a recoverable source and the limits of the original statement. An editor can then check each article, newsletter extract or social post against the recording.

This guide starts with an existing recording and follows it through to approved assets. Topic selection and the rest of production belong in [the wider AI-assisted content workflow](/insights/ai-assisted-content-workflow-expertise-guide).

## Confirm what the recording can be used for

Before uploading a transcript, check what was agreed with the speaker and anyone else whose information appears in it. Permission to record a conversation doesn't by itself settle how your team may publish, attribute or process it.

Record the intended public channels, whether the speaker can be named, any excluded subjects and the person who can resolve uncertainty. Check client references and confidential details separately. Confirm that the proposed AI tool and its handling of the material are approved for this use.

The [OAIC's guidance on commercially available AI products](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/guidance-on-privacy-and-the-use-of-commercially-available-ai-products) calls for due diligence, minimising personal information and accuracy controls with human oversight where Privacy Act obligations apply. Apply that to the actual transcript and tool. Remove unnecessary identifying details before processing and check who can access the inputs and outputs.

Recording-consent requirements vary across Australia. These editorial checks cover recording rights, public use and tool processing. If the legal position is uncertain, obtain advice for the relevant jurisdiction and use. Hold the material while permission or data handling remains unresolved.

## Verify the extracts that will carry the argument

Keep the original recording unchanged. Save transcript corrections in a separate, versioned file so a reviewer can see which wording was used to draft an asset.

Listen to the passages you intend to publish, including the surrounding question and answer. Check speaker identity, names, negation, figures, dates and uncertain words. A missing “don't” can reverse the recommendation. If you can't resolve a word from the audio, mark it uncertain and exclude it from a direct quote until clarified.

[NIST's Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) describes confabulation as confidently presented false content, including output that diverges from supplied input. It also recommends reviewing generated sources and citations. For interview reuse, Off Piste applies that concern by checking important extracts against the recording and retaining their source locations. The record below is our practical method, rather than a NIST-prescribed template.

Transcript verification establishes what the person said. Their claim about a market, regulation or product may still need independent evidence. [Evaluate supporting sources](/insights/evaluate-sources-website-content-guide) before publishing those assertions as facts. Keep an unsupported professional judgement attributed and within its original scope.

## Keep each usable statement attached to its source

Give an approved extract an ID that survives copying between documents. Store its location, conditions and permitted uses alongside the wording. A reviewer should be able to follow a draft sentence back to the relevant passage without searching an entire recording.

Use these fields for each evidence record.

- **Source ID** identifying the extract and its recording or transcript version.
- **Timestamp range** locating the passage in the original recording.
- **Speaker** with the agreed public attribution.
- **Verified wording** checked against the recording.
- **Qualification** stating the condition or limitation that must survive reuse.
- **Allowed use** recording approved channels and exclusions.
- **Corroboration status** separating verified speech from independently supported claims.
- **Derivative IDs** identifying assets that use the extract.
- **Approver** naming the person responsible and the approval version or date.

Then distinguish the wording mode in each derivative.

- **Direct quote** reproduces verified words with their meaning and context intact.
- **Attributed paraphrase** restates the speaker's point in new words while retaining its scope.
- **Editorial interpretation** makes the writer's own inference explicit and keeps it outside the speaker's quotation marks.

[Reuters' quotation standards](https://reutersagency.com/about/standards-values/) emphasise context and using paraphrase where appropriate. Off Piste adapts that principle by moving substantial rewrites into attributed paraphrase. A polished sentence shouldn't silently become something the expert supposedly said verbatim. Our expert-approval process is a separate business editorial choice.

Once verified, these records can feed [an approved content brief and source pack](/insights/ai-content-brief-source-pack-template). That handoff defines the writing assignment without making the writer reconstruct the interview evidence.

## Carry one qualified statement into three useful assets

The following example is entirely hypothetical. Alex is a fictional service designer, the recording and timestamp are invented, and all permissions and review decisions are illustrative. It demonstrates how to trace wording back to a source.

### The hypothetical source record

- **Source ID** INT-01, linked to fictional recording interview-01 and corrected transcript v1.
- **Timestamp range** 00:12:10–00:12:28, invented for this example.
- **Speaker** Alex, service designer, with named attribution permitted in the hypothetical agreement.
- **Verified wording** “A shorter enquiry form can help when we're asking for details we don't use. For complex projects, we may still need that information to assess fit.”
- **Qualification** The suggestion concerns unused information. Complex-project assessment may still require detail.
- **Allowed use** Article, newsletter and organic social post, subject to meaning review. No client references or performance claims.
- **Corroboration status** Illustrative professional judgement only. No measured effect on completion rates or lead quality has been established.
- **Derivative IDs** ART-01, NEWS-01 and SOC-01, all using transcript v1.
- **Approver** Alex for meaning and editor Jo for publication, fictional approval record v1 dated 11 November 2026.

A failed hypothetical social draft reads “Shorter enquiry forms always improve lead quality. Remove the extra questions.” Reject it. “Always” removes the condition about unused information and the exception for complex projects. “Improve lead quality” introduces an unsupported outcome. Merely replacing “always” with “can” would leave that second problem unresolved.

### Article derivative ART-01

The reader's job is to decide which enquiry questions deserve review. This hypothetical article paragraph uses attributed paraphrase followed by an explicit editorial interpretation.

> Alex suggests reviewing questions that collect information the team doesn't use. They also caution that complex projects may require those details to assess fit. Our practical reading is to check how each answer supports the assessment before deciding whether to remove the question.

The first two sentences preserve Alex's condition and exception. The final sentence belongs to the writer. Alex approves the meaning and Jo approves publication, with both hypothetical decisions recorded against INT-01 and transcript v1.

### Newsletter derivative NEWS-01

The reader's job is to recognise a question worth taking to their team. This hypothetical newsletter uses the complete direct quote.

> Reviewing your enquiry form? Service designer Alex offers this qualified starting point. “A shorter enquiry form can help when we're asking for details we don't use. For complex projects, we may still need that information to assess fit.”

The fictional review approves the complete quote and attribution against INT-01. If space is tight, shorten the introduction first. Retain enough of the extract to preserve the recommendation's meaning.

### Social derivative SOC-01

The reader's job is to identify one useful check before editing a form. This corrected hypothetical social post uses attributed paraphrase.

> Service designer Alex suggests reviewing enquiry questions that collect details your team doesn't use. Complex projects may still need that information to assess fit. Check what each answer is used for before removing a question.

The review approves the first two sentences as paraphrase of INT-01 and the final sentence as editorial advice. The post retains the qualification and removes the unsupported lead-quality promise. Keep the source ID and approval in the internal record, even when they aren't part of the public post.

## Give AI a bounded extraction and drafting task

Supply only approved extracts and the context needed to interpret them. Include source IDs, wording modes, qualifications and use restrictions. Asking a tool to make a transcript more persuasive leaves too much room to strengthen a cautious statement.

Specify what the reviewer needs back.

> Using only the approved extracts supplied, draft one newsletter paragraph for a service-business owner reviewing their enquiry form. Return the candidate wording with its source ID, wording mode and retained qualification. Copy direct quotes exactly. Put editorial interpretation outside quotation marks and identify it separately. Flag any unsupported claim or missing permission. Keep uncertainty visible and limit claims to the supplied evidence.

Treat the result as a candidate. [AP's standards around generative AI](https://www.ap.org/the-definitive-source/behind-the-news/standards-around-generative-ai/) treat generated output as material requiring editorial scrutiny. AP also sets its own stricter limits on creating publishable content with AI. The business practice here draws on its verification principle, without presenting AP's publishing policy as a rule for every organisation.

Check the returned source IDs too. A plausible identifier is useful only if the linked passage actually supports the sentence.

## Approve each derivative against its original context

Review the exact asset that will be published. Approval of the source extract doesn't automatically approve every headline, introduction or shortened version built from it. Those surrounding words can change what the reader takes away.

Check each derivative before approval.

- The source ID resolves to the correct passage and transcript version.
- Direct quotes match verified wording, and paraphrase or interpretation is identifiable.
- Conditions, exceptions and uncertainty survive in the asset itself.
- Factual assertions have appropriate corroboration or remain clearly bounded judgement.
- Attribution and the proposed channel fall within the permitted use.
- Named reviewers have approved this version for meaning and publication.

If the qualification can't fit in the proposed social format, record a hold decision and choose a format with room for it. A link to a longer article doesn't repair a misleading sentence in the post.

Keep expert meaning approval and editorial fact checking separate. An expert can confirm that a paraphrase represents their view while the editor still needs to verify a factual assertion. Use [the AI-assisted content quality audit](/insights/ai-assisted-content-quality-audit-guide) for the finished asset's wider publication review.

Record the source version and affected derivative IDs with each approval. If Alex later clarifies INT-01, the record points the editor to ART-01, NEWS-01 and SOC-01 for reassessment. Recheck the published wording and record any correction against the revised source.

## Publish only the formats that serve a reader

An article can explain a decision in depth. A newsletter can bring one relevant point to an existing audience. A social post can offer a useful check in a small space. Choose the format because it serves that reader's task and can carry the necessary context.

[Google's guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) warns that generating many pages without adding user value may violate its scaled-content-abuse policy. Each asset needs a useful purpose and an accurate argument.

Start with one extract from your next interview. Check it against the recording, record the condition that matters and approve one derivative that gives a reader something useful to do.
