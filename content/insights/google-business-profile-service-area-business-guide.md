## Start with how customers reach you

If you run a business from home and travel to every customer, should that address appear on Google? If customers visit your workshop and you also work at their properties, should you remove it? Those choices affect privacy, eligibility, and whether potential customers understand how the business operates.

The right Google Business Profile setup follows the real customer experience. Google's [service-area and hybrid business guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) defines a service-area business as one that visits or delivers to customers but doesn't serve them at its address. A hybrid business receives customers at its address and also visits or delivers to them.

This guide focuses on that classification and the location fields it controls. The broader [Google Business Profile optimisation guide](/insights/google-business-profile-guide) covers ongoing categories, reviews, posts, website alignment, and measurement.

## Choose the profile type that matches the business

Before editing an address or adding suburbs, identify what a customer can actually do at the location. A plumber who dispatches from home and never receives customers is a service-area business. A mobile dog groomer with no customer-facing premises is another. An accountant who regularly sees clients at a staffed office and also visits client sites may be a hybrid business.

The differences are easiest to check side by side.

| Profile type | Customer visits | Address shown | Signage and staffing | Service areas |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Storefront | Customers are served at the location | Yes | Real customer-facing operation during stated hours | Not required |
| Service-area | Business travels or delivers to customers | No | Base isn't presented as a customer location | Yes |
| Hybrid | Customers visit and business travels or delivers | Yes | Staffed customer-facing location with permanent signage | Yes |

The table describes profile behaviour, not three marketing options. Google's [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) require an address shown to customers to represent an eligible, real-world operation. A home office doesn't become a storefront because an appointment could theoretically be arranged there.

If customers don't visit the address, hide it. If they do visit during the stated hours and the premises meet Google's requirements, a storefront or hybrid setup may be accurate. Document the reason for the classification so a future owner or agency doesn't reverse it casually.

## Set the address and named areas correctly

Google's documented route is to open the Business Profile, choose **Edit profile**, then use the **Location** controls to manage the business address and service area. Interface wording can change and accounts don't always expose identical controls, so confirm the live labels before making the edit.

For a service-area business, remove the address from public display and enter the places the business genuinely serves. For a hybrid business, retain the eligible customer-facing address and add the appropriate service areas.

Google's [current service-area instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) use named cities, postal codes, or other available geographic areas rather than a radius. They allow up to 20 service areas. The same guidance says the overall area generally shouldn't extend beyond about two hours' driving time from the business base.

The 20-area maximum is a field limit. The two-hour statement is approximate operating guidance. Neither is a target to fill. A Perth electrician who reliably works in six nearby areas should list those six, not add distant places simply because more slots remain. A mobile allied-health provider should account for appointment length, travel time, and the territory the team can serve consistently.

Service areas are also separate from categories and the Services editor. Areas describe where the business serves customers. Categories classify the business, while services describe its offer. Use the [categories and services guide](/insights/google-business-profile-categories-services-guide) when those fields are the actual decision.

Before saving, capture the current address display, service areas, verification state, and profile ownership. Record the intended change and its operational reason. That baseline will make an unexpected result easier to diagnose.

## Treat a material edit as an eligibility check

An address or business-type change is more than a copy edit. It asks Google to represent the operation differently, so prepare the evidence that supports the new setup.

Google selects the [verification methods available to a business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en). Depending on the method offered, it may ask for evidence about the location, equipment, signage, or the person's authority to represent the business. Don't promise the team a particular verification channel or approval time.

Make sure the profile owner can access the account before the change. Keep business registration, invoices, utility records, branded vehicles or equipment, and premises evidence available where they truthfully apply. A hybrid or storefront location needs to operate as the customer-facing location it claims to be. Evidence should substantiate the real business rather than be assembled to manufacture eligibility.

After saving, review the profile rather than assuming the change is complete. Check whether Google requests verification and whether the public result reflects the intended address setting and coverage.

## Represent each real location accurately

A wider working territory doesn't justify an extra profile in each suburb. Google's [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say a service-area business should generally have one profile for the central office or location. Another profile needs another eligible location with separate staff and a genuine operation.

Virtual offices aren't eligible merely because they provide a desirable address. A co-working address also isn't suitable when the business doesn't maintain the required presence, signage, and customer-facing operation there. A residential base can support an eligible service-area business, but its address should be hidden when customers aren't served there.

Consider a hypothetical cleaning company that dispatches two teams from one depot across metropolitan Perth. The teams' coverage doesn't create several branches. One accurate profile describes the central operation. If the company later opens a separately staffed location that genuinely serves its own area, use the [new business location search visibility checklist](/insights/new-business-location-search-visibility-checklist) to assess the wider launch before creating another profile.

## Use service areas as customer information

Named service areas tell customers where the business operates. Google documents local visibility through other factors, rather than treating these entries as a control that expands a ranking radius.

Google says local results are mainly based on [relevance, distance, and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). Adding 20 suburbs doesn't remove distance from that system or guarantee visibility in each selected place. It can instead create mismatched enquiries if the business can't serve the territory reliably.

Off Piste's practical judgement is to select the smallest accurate set that explains the viable footprint, then review enquiry quality. If calls repeatedly arrive from places the team can't reach profitably, revisit the public coverage and website promise.

## Align the website with the operating footprint

The Business Profile, website pages, and structured data are separate surfaces. They should agree on material facts without being treated as the same field.

A service page can explain what the business offers across its territory. A useful location page goes further by helping customers in a specific place understand service availability, logistics, relevant work, and the next step. Google's [spam policy on doorway abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#doorway-abuse) warns against pages made to rank for similar queries and funnel people to one destination. Swapping a suburb name across near-duplicate pages isn't local proof.

Create a location page only when it has distinct customer value. That might include services genuinely available there, local project evidence, travel or scheduling constraints, and information that changes the decision. Keep this website work subordinate to the truthful operating model.

Structured data describes visible website content. It doesn't control Business Profile coverage. The [LocalBusiness schema guide for locations and service areas](/insights/local-business-schema-locations-service-areas-guide) explains that separate implementation.

## Check the profile after the change

Review a material edit in order so privacy or verification problems don't disappear inside a general performance discussion.

1. Confirm the address is hidden for a service-area business or visible only for an eligible storefront or hybrid location.
2. Check that every named service area is accurate, reachable, and spelled as Google displays it.
3. Review ownership and verification status, then complete only the verification method Google offers.
4. View the public profile as a customer and confirm its address, hours, phone, website, and service information tell one consistent story.
5. Check relevant website pages and structured data for contradictory location or coverage claims.
6. Record the edit date and review whether later enquiries come from appropriate areas and match the services the business wants to sell.

If the profile becomes pending, restricted, missing, or visible only for branded searches, work through the [Business Profile visibility diagnostic](/insights/google-business-profile-not-showing-diagnostic-guide) before making further speculative edits. For outcome review, [measure Business Profile performance through qualified leads](/insights/measure-google-business-profile-performance-qualified-leads) rather than treating profile interactions as revenue.

## Match the setup to the operation

The profile should describe how the business actually serves customers. Hide an address customers can't visit, show a real customer-facing location when it qualifies, and list only the areas the team can reliably serve.

That accuracy protects privacy and eligibility while giving customers a more useful expectation. When the situation involves disputed eligibility, several genuine locations, duplicate profiles, or recovery after a damaging edit, a coordinated [SEO engagement](/services/seo) can help align the profile, website, and measurement without inventing local presence.
