# Website Claim Substantiation SERP Validation

Date: 2026-10-07

Publishing timezone: Australia/Perth

Owner: Lara and Off Piste pipeline agent

## Search job

Validate whether a preventive, pre-publication claim substantiation workflow is distinct from academic website citation queries, broad citation-worthy content, source evaluation and retrospective evidence auditing.

## Queries checked

- how to substantiate marketing claims
- website claim substantiation checklist
- how to prove advertising claims
- evidence for marketing claims
- claim approval workflow
- how to review website claims before publishing
- Australian Consumer Law website claims

The environment provided a logged-out web-search sample rather than a personalised browser session. Search volume, advertising density, People Also Ask and AI Overview presence could not be reliably measured. No configured keyword-volume tool was available in the repository, so no search-volume conclusion is recorded or used in the article.

## Observed result shape

- Australian regulator and government sources were prominent for advertising, consumer-law and evidence queries. Results included the ACCC, TGA, Ahpra, ACMA, Food Standards Australia New Zealand and business.gov.au.
- The ACCC false or misleading claims page directly addressed accuracy, reasonable grounds, proof, omissions, fine print and overall impression across websites and other business communications.
- Sector-specific results commonly used self-assessment checklists and required substantiation before publication, particularly for therapeutic and health claims.
- Legal explainers and operational substantiation checklists appeared alongside regulator guidance.
- Broad queries also surfaced academic claim-verification results and US advertising guidance. Those are adjacent intents and should not define the Australian legal frame.
- Academic “how to cite a website” intent was excluded from the article angle.

## Editorial decision

Keep the approved title, **How to Substantiate Website Claims Before Publishing**. The wording matches the practical preventive job and avoids the academic meaning of citation. Use the ACCC as the governing Australian frame. Use FTC guidance only as a clearly labelled US comparison.

The article should remain a draft-stage approval workflow. It should hand source assessment to the existing source-evaluation guide and hand live-site diagnosis to the existing evidence-audit guide.
