What Google AI Overviews and AI Mode Mean for SEO

Google AI Overviews and AI Mode have moved the search question beyond rankings alone. Google SGE was the earlier name, but the practical issue is current: can Google understand, trust and surface your business while a buyer researches a service?

Lara at Off Piste Studio

March 6, 2026 (Updated October 25, 2026)

Share

AI Overviews and AI Mode changed the search journey

Search Generative Experience, or Google SGE, was the early label. The live products are AI Overviews and AI Mode. They can summarise information, connect a searcher to supporting websites and extend one question into a longer research session.

For a founder or marketing lead, the practical question is whether Google can find the right page, understand the offer, trust its evidence and surface it while a buyer compares options.

Google describes AI Overviews as a way to get the gist of a complex question, with links for further exploration. AI Mode is designed for nuanced questions, reasoning, comparisons and follow-ups. An Overview can also hand the searcher into a conversational exchange in AI Mode. Google said that handoff was live worldwide in its Search I/O 2026 update.

The commercial consequence is a less linear path. A buyer may learn from a generated answer, open a source, refine the question and search a brand later. A single ranking position tells only part of that story.

One question can create several retrieval needs

Google's generative AI optimisation guidance explains that AI Overviews and AI Mode may use query fan-out. The system can issue several related searches across subtopics and data sources before developing a response. Google also describes retrieval-augmented generation as one of the techniques these features may use.

A buyer asking whether an SEO agency is suitable could prompt retrieval around location, services, sector experience, pricing, proof, risks and comparisons. They may type none of those separate searches, and website owners receive no trace of Google's inferred queries. Google needs useful pages and evidence beyond the exact phrase in the search box.

Coherent coverage serves these retrieval needs without duplicating a page for every imagined subquery. Service pages, case studies and guidance should each have a clear role, then connect through descriptive internal links. Our guide to planning content for AI Mode query fan-out covers that implementation work and treats inferred queries as hypotheses rather than keyword data.

Search foundations determine eligibility

Google says its AI features are rooted in core Search ranking and quality systems. Its technical requirements for AI features say a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet. Standard search requirements apply. Google requires no AI-only markup layer, llms.txt file or special schema.

If a proposal still sells one of those items as a Google-specific shortcut, use our guide to evaluating which AI search tactics deserve budget before approving the work.

Important pages must be crawlable and indexable, with useful content visible on the page. Internal links should make related services and evidence easy to find. Structured data should agree with what a reader can see. Page experience and accessibility matter when a person follows a source link.

Eligibility is only the starting point. It doesn't guarantee that Google will crawl, index, select or show a page. If an eligible site is absent while other sources appear, use the AI Overview citation diagnostic to separate an access problem from weak query fit or weak source value. If the structure itself is unclear, the structured content guide shows how to repair headings, page roles, visible evidence and machine-readable support.

If Google already shows the business but states a false, stale or unsupported fact, follow the wrong AI Overview correction guide. It separates source repair, feedback and recrawl requests so each is used for its actual job.

For a service business, a technically eligible page can enter consideration. A clear page with credible proof gives both Google and the buyer more reason to keep it there.

If important copy or links depend on JavaScript, audit JavaScript rendering on important service pages to collect evidence and define a repair pass condition.

Google is making source discovery more explicit

On 15 October 2026, Google announced more ways to connect with sources from Search. The update described clearer inline links and more context about linked pages. It also introduced dedicated official-website sections, carousels for timely articles and clickable creator profiles in relevant AI Mode and AI Overview experiences.

Those treatments can make a source easier for a buyer to inspect. Their appearance can still vary by query, device, country and rollout state. We couldn't verify that every treatment is available for every Australian search as at 21 October 2026.

Google presents these as discovery and interface changes. It doesn't describe them as ranking factors or promise that an eligible page will be selected. More visible links create an opportunity, not a guarantee.

Off Piste's practical interpretation is that source quality now has another commercial moment. When Google exposes an official website, author or supporting page, accurate business information and visible authorship give the buyer more to verify. Current evidence and useful source pages can make the next click worthwhile. If Google misunderstands the organisation behind the content, start with how AI search understands a business. If the site is eligible but absent from supporting links, use the AI Overview citation diagnostic.

Generic summaries are easy to compress because many websites make the same points. Original examples, named expertise, specific service boundaries, current pricing context, first-hand observations and documented outcomes are more useful to a buyer. They also give a generated response something precise to attribute. The guide to creating citation-worthy AI search content develops that evidence standard in more detail.

Put proof beside the decision it supports. A case study should explain what changed. A service page should state who the work suits and where its limits sit. An article should distinguish experience from inference.

Reporting and controls show different parts of the problem

Google's Generative AI performance report documentation says Search Console can show clicks and impressions by page, country, device and date. Google also documents aggregation, access and scope limits. The report shows exposure within covered Google experiences. It doesn't reveal a private retrieval trace or prove why Google selected a page.

Google's announcement about controls and insights for website owners describes a separate property-level control. It affects whether a site is eligible to appear in and help ground covered generative AI Search features. Google distinguishes it from narrower snippet controls and Google-Extended.

The control has a real tradeoff. Google says an opted-out site won't receive traffic or impressions from those generative AI features, while the choice isn't used as a ranking signal outside them. The AI Overviews opt-out decision guide explains how to weigh visibility, content use, measurement and commercial exposure before changing the setting.

The report answers a narrower question than a complete marketing dashboard. It can't tell you whether the description was persuasive, whether a later branded search was influenced by that exposure or whether a lead was worth pursuing. Use the Generative AI performance report operating guide to configure that Google-specific baseline and interpret its dimensions. Our framework for measuring AI search visibility combines the report with citation review, prompt sampling, analytics and sales evidence.

Traffic needs a commercial reading

AI features can change clicks, but their effect varies by query. A generic definition may be satisfied inside the result. A buyer choosing an adviser, checking a provider's work or deciding whether a service fits still needs evidence and judgement. That person may visit a source immediately, return through a branded search or arrive later on a high-intent page.

Sessions and click-through rate remain useful, but they need context. Watch generative AI impressions, branded demand, visits to commercial pages, qualified enquiries, assisted conversions and what prospects say in sales conversations. Those signals help distinguish lost informational clicks from influence that moved elsewhere in the journey.

If traffic or CTR has already changed, the AI Overviews traffic-drop diagnostic provides the right next step. It tests reporting, ranking, demand, feature exposure and conversion evidence before assigning a cause. That is safer than treating every decline after a product rollout as proof that AI Overviews caused it.

Agentic Search remains an emerging direction

Google is extending Search from answering questions toward completing some tasks. Its Search I/O 2026 announcements included information agents, expanded booking capabilities and custom experiences built inside Search.

Availability matters. Google's May announcement said local-services booking and calling capabilities were rolling out in the United States, while custom Antigravity experiences were due to start with US Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. The announcement didn't establish those capabilities as generally available to Australian service businesses. It described the conversational handoff from AI Overviews to AI Mode as worldwide.

Australian operators should watch agentic Search as an emerging product direction. Clear service data, accessible interfaces, current availability and well-structured proof are useful website investments today. A feature-specific playbook can wait until the buyer use case and Australian availability are clear.

Choose the next job from the evidence

The right response depends on where the problem appears. Start with AI search measurement to establish whether the business is visible. If Google can access the site but rarely uses it as a source, work through the citation diagnostic. For a performance change, use the traffic-drop diagnostic.

When one buyer question spans several topics, move into query-family content planning. When the concern is whether Google should use the content at all, make the inclusion-control decision before changing Search Console settings.

Some problems cross reporting, content architecture, technical eligibility and internal linking. That calls for joined-up SEO planning. If the constraint is the template, accessibility, proof presentation or page architecture, website design is the more direct route.

AI Overviews and AI Mode raise the value of a website that's easy to understand and verify. Identify whether the current constraint is eligibility, evidence, measurement, coverage or control, then fix that specific job.
