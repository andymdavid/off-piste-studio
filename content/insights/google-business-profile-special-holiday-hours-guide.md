## Make the next customer visit predictable

A customer checks your Google Business Profile before leaving home, then opens your website to book. If one says you're open and the other offers appointments during a closure, they still have to guess which information to trust.

Give each hours change an approved schedule, a publisher and a public check. Customers need to know when they can visit, call or book.

This guide covers holiday hours and exceptional closures. For ownership, categories and the rest of your profile, use [the wider Google Business Profile guide](/insights/google-business-profile-guide).

## Choose the route that matches the operation

Before editing a schedule, check whether your business should publish hours. [Google's business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say businesses operating only by appointment are among those that shouldn't provide hours. Keep the profile faithful to that operating model. Make booking availability and contact arrangements clear on the practice's website and booking service.

For businesses that should display hours, choose the field according to what has changed.

| Situation | Route | Check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Weekly or seasonal change | Edit regular hours | Follow [Google's hours guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en). |
| Dated exception | Special hours | [Preserve regular hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en). |
| Separate service schedule | More hours | [Service hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9876800?hl=en) require regular hours and generally sit within primary hours. |
| Closure up to six consecutive days | Special hours | [Special hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en) and [closure guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300196) agree. |
| Closure longer than seven consecutive days | Temporarily closed | Follow [Google's temporary closure guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300196). |
| Closure of exactly seven consecutive days | Check conflicting guidance | See the seven-day caveat below. |
| Indefinite or off-season closure | Temporarily closed | The [closure instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300196) cover indefinite and off-season closures. |
| Operation only by appointment | Use booking information | Apply the [appointment-only exception](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) before configuring hours. |

The seven-day boundary needs care. At the source check for this article, Google's Special hours page directs closures of seven or more days to temporary closure. Its closure page says more than seven days, with seven or fewer using Special hours. For exactly seven days, check the current pages and the prompts in your account. If they don't resolve the choice, ask Google Business Profile support before publishing that status. We haven't inspected your account interface.

When the weekly timetable itself changes, [Google's regular-hours instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300403?hl=en) explain separate opening periods for breaks and a dedicated 24-hour setting. Confirm whether the new timetable is genuinely the ongoing schedule before replacing the baseline.

## Record the approved dates before editing

Start with whoever can approve the actual operation. That might be the owner, practice manager or location manager. Before updating the profile, the person maintaining it needs approved details of customer access, staffing and bookings.

We recommend keeping one dated record for the change. A holiday name alone leaves room for mistakes about the year, location or additional days the business has chosen to close.

- Record the location, timezone and exact calendar dates, including the year.
- Specify each opening interval or all-day closure and the intended reopening date.
- Confirm what customers can book, collect or arrange by phone during that period.
- Name the approver and publisher, with the website and booking changes they own.
- Set dates for the public check and the reopening check.

Share the approved record with everyone maintaining the profile, website and booking system.

## Publish special hours for each affected date

For an eligible business, follow [Google's Special hours editing instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en) with the approved record beside you.

1. Open the correct Business Profile, then select **Edit profile** and **Hours**.
2. Edit **Special hours**. Review a suggested date, add a date or select an existing one.
3. Enter the opening and closing times, or mark the date **Closed**. Add another interval if needed.
4. Repeat for the affected dates, then select **Save** and review the saved entries.

Google recommends confirming holiday hours even when unchanged. Its instructions also require **Open with main hours** for Special hours to display. Apply the appointment-only exception first.

Check each date against your approved record. A correct opening time attached to the wrong date still gives customers the wrong instruction. Leave enough time before the change to inspect the public result and resolve discrepancies.

For a closure requiring the temporary status, [Google's documented route](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300196) is **Edit profile**, **Hours**, then edit the main **Hours** setting, choose **Temporarily closed** and save. Record who will reopen the profile when the business returns.

## Check the website and booking path

Follow the website link from the profile to the page a customer actually reaches. Check its visible hours, closure notice and contact instructions against the approved dates. Then follow the booking or enquiry path far enough to see whether someone could arrange a visit that the business can't fulfil.

Update and check the profile, website and booking system separately. [Google's LocalBusiness documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) supports opening hours and seasonal periods in website structured data. If the site already publishes those facts, [align location facts and LocalBusiness markup](/insights/local-business-schema-locations-service-areas-guide) with the visible information. Keep technical implementation with the person responsible for that site.

You might need to coordinate with a supplier who controls the location page while a separate booking platform continues accepting closed-day appointments. Once the approved record identifies the conflict, [coordinated local search and website support](/services/seo) can help organise the changes and checks across those systems.

## A short holiday closure in practice

Consider a fictional Perth repair shop, Banksia Repairs. Its normal customer-facing hours are Monday to Friday, 9 am to 5 pm. The owner approves opening from 9 am to noon on 24 December 2026, closure from 25 through 28 December inclusive, and normal hours from 29 December. These fictional operating decisions illustrate the process. Each business needs to approve its own holiday arrangements.

The manager, Priya, records the dates in Australia/Perth time and approves the customer arrangements. Sam, who maintains the profile and website, publishes the shortened opening on 24 December and marks each date from 25 to 28 December closed in Special hours. The normal weekly schedule stays in place.

The shop's booking administrator blocks collection appointments after noon on 24 December and throughout the closure. Sam adds a location-page notice with the same dates and the return on 29 December. Priya also confirms what happens to enquiries received while the shop is closed, so the notice can set an accurate expectation about replies.

In this example, Sam schedules a public check for 18 December, a final check on 24 December and a reopening check on 29 December. The first check follows the profile through to the location page and booking calendar. Any mismatch goes back into the record with a person responsible for correcting it.

## Verify the public result and resolve missing hours

Seeing the saved entry in the profile manager's interface confirms it's there. You still need to check what a customer can see. We recommend this sequence when Special hours appear to be missing or inconsistent.

1. Compare saved dates and times with the approved record, including the year and all-day closures.
2. Check hours eligibility and the main-hours setting using the linked Google guidance. Keep appointment-only operations on their appropriate route.
3. Inspect the public profile on Search and Maps. Record what each view shows and when you checked it.
4. Follow the public website and booking links. Compare the available dates, notices and contact arrangements with the same record.
5. Log discrepancies, assign the correction and set a recheck. If the configuration remains unclear, use Google's support route with the details you've observed.

Public views may differ after an edit. Record the saved configuration and public observations separately, with check times, so you can track what changes.

If the whole profile is absent, restricted or otherwise unavailable, [diagnose why your Business Profile isn't showing](/insights/google-business-profile-not-showing-diagnostic-guide). That broader problem needs its own investigation before an hours-only check can be useful.

## Confirm reopening and close the record

For dated exceptions, check the intended return to normal hours and remove expired website notices. Confirm that the booking system accepts the right appointments again. In the fictional shop's record, Priya verifies on 29 December that customers can see the normal hours and arrange a collection during them.

If you used **Temporarily closed**, follow [Google's reopening instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300571). Open **Edit profile**, select **Hours** and edit the main **Hours** setting. For a business that displays hours, choose **Open with main hours**, set the applicable days and times, then save. Google also documents **Open with no main hours** for reopening without displaying hours. Choose the setting that reflects the real operation and its eligibility.

Close the record after the named owner has checked the reopened public profile, location page and booking or contact path. Keep the check date and any unresolved discrepancy for the next update.
