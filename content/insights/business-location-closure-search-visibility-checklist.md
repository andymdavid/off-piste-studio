## A closed branch is still a customer journey

A customer books time off, follows directions to your branch and finds the doors shut. The business still operates elsewhere, but an old listing and location page sent them to the wrong place. The missed appointment is only part of the problem. The customer now has to work out who to call and whether another branch can help.

This checklist covers one approved permanent branch closure while the organisation continues. Make the change clear wherever customers encounter that location. Consistent information also helps establish [how search systems understand your business](/insights/how-ai-search-understands-your-business), although no checklist can guarantee a particular search result.

Consider a fictional Australian service business, Banksia Home Services, with branches in Fremantle and Joondalup. Fremantle is closing permanently. Joondalup will continue to serve some customers, but it isn't a replacement for every appointment or service. The team needs to tell people what is changing and offer a useful next step.

## Confirm what is changing

Get the decision in writing before changing public records. A permanent closure means this branch is ending its operations. A temporary interruption needs a different status. Under [Google's closure guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300196), use special hours for a closure of seven days or less and temporary closure for a longer, seasonal or indefinite interruption.

A business moving to new premises needs a relocation assessment. Start with the [moving premises checklist](/insights/business-relocation-search-visibility-checklist), then check [Google's current Business Profile policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416) and applicable location guidance for the actual circumstances before changing the profile. A suspended profile is a separate access or eligibility issue. If the branch remains open, use the [suspension appeal guide](/insights/google-business-profile-suspension-appeal-guide) instead of marking it closed.

## Build the closure record

Create a shared register before anyone edits a listing. Make sure the website owner, branch manager and customer team are working from the same decision. Record:

- The approved closure date, last day of service and any changes to bookings.
- Customer contact details and alternatives, including which services remain available elsewhere.
- The profile URL, location page, booking links, phone numbers, maps and structured data references.
- Directory listings, data suppliers and other records that show the branch.
- An owner, due date, approved action and verification evidence for each change.

Banksia's operations lead confirms the final Fremantle appointment date. The marketing lead inventories the page and listings, while the customer team confirms which enquiries Joondalup can handle. Each names a backup. In a larger network, the [franchise and multi-location governance guide](/insights/franchise-multi-location-entity-governance-guide) can help establish who approves a change and who carries it out.

## Prepare the customer message

Tell customers what is closing, when it closes and what they can do next. A useful notice might say that Banksia Fremantle closes on a specified date, explain what happens to existing bookings and provide a working contact for further help. Only recommend Joondalup where the service and travel implications make sense. Give customers an alternative contact when they cannot use that branch.

Coordinate the notice with staff, affected customers and booking systems. Check confirmation emails, advertisements, social profiles, signs and phone routing. Make sure someone answers the number or updates its recorded message after closure. Schedule public changes around the approved effective date and check that customers can no longer book appointments at the inactive branch. Adapt the sequence to your business and its customers.

## Update the Business Profile

When the branch has permanently closed, an authorised manager can mark its profile permanently closed using the steps in [Google's closure guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300196). Google says the profile may still appear in Search and Maps for people who search for it, with a closed status. Record the submission date, then check the public profile separately and save evidence of the status shown.

Removing profile content and managers from an account is a different action. [Google's account removal guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4669092) says that removal ends management through that account and does not guarantee the business disappears from Search and Maps. Review the consequences and retain appropriate access for monitoring before deciding whether removal is necessary. Don't assume either action guarantees removal or preserves reviews.

## Decide what the location page should do

Choose the page response based on what customers need after closure. Banksia may keep its Fremantle page as an accurate closure notice while customers still need booking and contact information. The page should state the closure date, remove claims that the branch is open and explain relevant alternatives. Assign someone to review the notice later and check whether customers still need it.

If a genuinely relevant replacement page exists, consider a permanent redirect to it. The destination should answer the reason someone followed the old link. For example, a dedicated page explaining how former Fremantle customers can obtain the same service may be relevant. Sending everyone to an unrelated homepage or another branch without context can leave them stranded. [Google's site move guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes) warns that redirects to irrelevant destinations can be treated as soft 404s.

If the page is removed and there is no similar replacement, return a meaningful 404 or 410 response. [Google's crawling error guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/troubleshoot-crawling-errors) explains these responses for pages that are gone. Test the actual HTTP response, not just the message displayed in a browser.

Whatever you choose, review location finders, navigation, internal links, sitemap entries, embedded maps and booking links. Remove references that present the branch as operating. Update structured data to match the visible, current information and avoid leaving an active location represented after it has closed. Check shared templates as well as the individual page, since a footer or schema feed can repeat old details across the site.

## Correct external records and check the result

Start with records the business controls, then work through data suppliers and the independent listings customers are most likely to use. Search for the branch name, address and phone number to find records outside the original inventory. The [third-party business signals audit](/insights/third-party-brand-signals-ai-search-audit) offers a wider method for finding and assessing those sources.

For each listing, record the requested correction, submission date, responsible person and public result. Some directories may require a request or supporting information. A submitted request isn't proof that the public record has changed. Keep the discrepancy open until someone checks it, and escalate stale or incorrect records through the provider's available process.

As an operating routine, Banksia could check the key surfaces shortly after the closure, again a few weeks later and at a later agreed review. The owner should test the page response and booking journey, inspect the live Business Profile and revisit priority listings. Record the date and evidence for each observation, and choose a cadence that suits your team. Google does not require this schedule. Use the [search and AI monitoring framework](/insights/entity-trust-monitoring-search-ai-framework) to incorporate unresolved discrepancies into ongoing checks.

## Put ownership around the handover

Before the last day, walk through the customer journey from a search result to a booking or phone call. Give each unresolved item an owner and a next review date. That gives the remaining team a way to follow up after the branch team has moved on.

If website changes, location data and external listings sit with different people, an [SEO conversation](/services/seo) can help coordinate the work and identify gaps in the handover.
